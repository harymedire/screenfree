SCREENFREE · Weekly System #4 · getscreenfree.com Intro

Welcome

Weekly System #4

This week is all about the senses. Across seven days, your child moves through every main sensory channel — hearing, smell, touch, movement, sight, taste, and calming the body. Each day builds one very specific skill:

  • listening attention and sound discrimination
  • scent recognition and speech
  • tactile sensitivity
  • rhythm and body coordination
  • focus and emotional literacy
  • taste, texture, and a healthy relationship with food
  • self-regulation, calming, and closeness

Every day has a stronger WOW mission, a medium variation, and an easy back-up — so you can match the play to your child's age, mood, and energy. The materials are always close at hand: paper, a spoon, water, a few spices, a towel, music, a mirror, one bite of food, a blanket.

This isn't a task list.
This is 7 days when every sense gets attention — and when your child learns to notice, name, and calm their own body and feelings.

1

What Do You Hear?

The room is quiet — figure out what's happening.

The room is quiet. Someone is making a sound.

⚡ WOW Mission

Secret Sounds

Materials: paper, a spoon, a glass, a door, water, a box — any safe items from around the house.

How to play

Your child sits with their back turned or eyes closed. Make one sound at a time: crumple paper, tap a spoon on a glass, pour water, open a door, shake a box. Pause after each: “What do you think that was? Quiet or loud? Near or far?” If they don't guess, show the object and repeat the sound. The goal isn't being right — it's listening carefully. Then switch roles — your child makes the sound, you guess. That's usually when the laughter starts, because they love “knowing the secret.”

🔧 Medium

Where's the Sound Hiding?

Hide a quiet sound source somewhere in the room — a phone on low, a ticking clock, a box of beads, or a spoon you tap behind a pillow. Your child listens and looks for where it's coming from. “Listen first. Don't run yet. Where do your ears say to go?” For younger kids, keep the sound close and clear; for older ones, hide it behind a chair or in another corner.

🌿 Easy

Quiet Challenge

Sit together: “Let's hear what the house is saying.” Listen for 20–30 seconds and name what you hear: a car outside, the fridge, water, a bird, footsteps. A great little reset when your child is overstimulated — it pulls them out of the noise and into listening.

2

Scent in a Jar

The nose is a detective — today we smell first.

The nose is a detective. Today we smell first.

⚡ WOW Mission

Scent Detective

Materials: 3–5 small jars or cups, napkins, coffee, lemon, cinnamon, soap, vanilla, or other gentle scents.

How to play

Place one scent in each jar. Cover with a napkin so your child can't see what's inside. They smell one at a time and describe it. Don't push for the exact word — what matters is that they use words like: “sweet,” “strong,” “like a cookie,” “like the bathroom,” “like mom.” Model the language: “This smells fresh to me.” “This reminds me of the kitchen.” Then open the jar: “The detective nose got it!” Skip strong chemicals, perfume held under the nose, and hot spices — keep everything gentle and safe.

🔧 Medium

Find the Match

Make pairs: two coffee jars, two cinnamon, two lemon. Your child smells and finds the two that “smell the same.” “Is this one the same as the first? Which is stronger? Which one do you like best?” For younger kids, just 2 pairs; for older ones, 4 scents.

🌿 Easy

Smell Walk Around the House

No jars — just walk through the house and find scents: the kitchen, bathroom, clean laundry, fruit, bread, soap, tea. “What smells in here? Nice or strong? Does it remind you of anything?” The easiest version, takes just a few minutes.

3

Path Under Your Feet

Today the feet become eyes — we walk and feel.

Today the feet become eyes.

⚡ WOW Mission

Sensory Path

Materials: a towel, cardboard, sponge, sock, foil, plastic bag, pillow, cloth — safe textures from around the house.

How to play

Lay out 5–7 different surfaces on the floor. Your child walks across slowly, barefoot or in socks. Each step is a new sensation: soft, cold, rough, smooth, crinkly. Don't over-explain — just give words to what your child is feeling: “This is soft. This crinkles. Here your foot sinks a little.” If they don't want to go barefoot, let them touch with a hand first. Add a story: “This is a forest path. This is a cold bridge. This is a soft cloud.” The tactile experience directly supports confidence in movement and self-regulation — a child who feels the floor feels their body better, too.

🔧 Medium

Guess Without Looking

Tuck a few textures under a towel or into a box. Your child reaches in and describes before peeking. “Soft or hard? Smooth or rough? Big or small?” If they don't know the word, offer two choices. For older kids: “Find something soft.” “Find something that crinkles.”

🌿 Easy

Warm and Cold

Get two safe items ready: a warm cloth and a metal spoon or a cold cup. Your child touches and picks: “What's cold? What's warm?” Quick and ideal for younger kids — introduces basic sensory words without overstimulating.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #4 · getscreenfree.com Day 4 / 7
4

Music That Moves You

Listen with your whole body.

Sound enters the body. Listen with your whole body.

⚡ WOW Mission

The Dance That Changes

Materials: music, a song you sing, hand-clapping, or a rhythm game.

How to play

Switch up the kind of music or rhythm — fast, slow, quiet, loud, happy, calm. Your child moves the way the sound “asks” them to. Fast music = run or jump. Slow = snail. Quiet = tiptoe. Loud = big movements. Talk it through: “This music is fast — what are your feet doing?” “Now it's quiet — can we walk like cats?” “Now slow — like we're underwater.” The goal isn't “correct” dancing but linking sound, emotion, and body. Your child learns that rhythm can change movement, and movement can change how the body feels.

🔧 Medium

Freeze!

Music plays, your child moves. When the music stops: “Stop! Statue!” They hold still for a few seconds, then keep going. A great self-control workout — they learn to stop the body on cue, which is the foundation for following directions later. For younger kids, keep the pause very short. For older ones add: “Freeze like a frog!” “Freeze like a tree!”

🌿 Easy

Follow My Beat

Clap a simple rhythm: tap-tap, pause. Your child repeats it. Then tap on knees, the table, the floor. Don't overcomplicate — listen, wait, repeat. If they can't yet, do hand-over-hand or just let them watch.

5

Mirror

The movement that reflects back.

I move. You see me.

⚡ WOW Mission

Mirror Game

Materials: parent and child, a little open space.

How to play

Stand facing each other. Slowly make a movement: lift a hand, touch your nose, nod, squat, spread your arms, make a silly face. Your child copies you like a mirror. The key is a slow pace — kids 2–4 are still learning to plan a movement, watch someone else, and control their body all at once. Then switch roles: your child leads, you follow. That's where it builds: “Someone is following me. My movement matters.” Add a twist: “The mirror is laughing.” “The mirror is a robot.” “The mirror is sleepy.” “The mirror turned into a frog.” No corrections, no critique — just following.

🔧 Medium

Mirror of Emotions

Make a face: happy, sad, surprised, angry, sleepy. Your child copies it and names it. “What does a happy face look like? What does an angry face do? Can a sad face get a hug?” If they don't want to “act emotions,” use a stuffed animal: “What does the bear look like when he's surprised?”

🌿 Easy

One Movement

Just one movement per round: lift your hand, clap, wave, touch your head. Your child copies. Perfect for younger kids or tired days — three easy reps beats ten frustrating ones.

6

Kitchen of Tastes

The tongue explores — taste, sound, texture.

The tongue explores.

⚡ WOW Mission

Little Taste Tester

Materials: a plate, 4–5 safe small bites of food: apple, banana, cheese, cracker, yogurt, cucumber, bread.

How to play

Put little bites of familiar food on a plate. Your child tries them one at a time and describes each. Don't push them to eat what they don't want — the goal is exploring, not “finish it all.” Lead with questions: “Sweet? Salty? Sour? Does it crunch? Soft? Dry or wet?” A younger child can just point or make a face. Add a “tester's rating”: thumbs up, thumbs sideways, thumbs down. This way your child learns they have a right to their own experience of food, which long-term supports a healthier relationship with eating. Caution: allergies, choking-hazard foods, and small bites — keep everything soft, small, and age-appropriate.

🔧 Medium

Guess the Taste

Your child closes their eyes or looks away. Offer a small, familiar bite — they guess what it is. “What do you think it is? Same as before? What do you hear when you bite?” If they don't like closing their eyes, let them smell first, then try.

🌿 Easy

Food Has Words

No guessing. During a meal, name the qualities: banana is soft, the apple crunches, yogurt is cold, bread is dry, cheese is salty. Sensory words land best in real moments, not as a lesson.

7

Little Animals Settle Down

The week ends quietly — the body settles.

The body has been busy. Now it learns to settle.

⚡ WOW Mission

Animal Yoga

Materials: a soft surface, rug, or blanket.

How to play

Don't call it “exercise” — call it “the animal game.” Cat: on all fours, arch the back. Snake: on the belly, slowly lift the head. Dog: hands and feet on the floor, hips up. Frog: squat and hop a little. Tree: balance on one leg with help. Stone: curl up and hold still. Hold each pose for a few seconds. Speak softly: “The cat is stretching.” “The snake breathes slowly.” “The stone is resting.” Don't push for the “right” form — the goal isn't yoga technique, it's body awareness, balance, and settling. At the end, lie down together with a hand on the belly: “Belly goes up… belly goes down.” The simplest form of breath regulation for a little one.

🔧 Medium

Animal Sequence

A row of 3 animals: cat, frog, stone. Your child remembers and copies. For older kids add a 4th pose; for younger ones keep it at 2. “First the cat. Then the frog. Then the stone goes to sleep.” If they forget, show again without correcting.

🌿 Easy

The Stone Rests

Your child curls up like a little stone or lies on a blanket. Speak softly: “The stone is still. The stone is resting. The stone hears the room.” A perfect closer before bed or after a busy day — no goal beyond settling and closeness.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #4 · getscreenfree.com SOS ideas

Ready for in-the-moment

SOS ideas
when your child asks for a screen

When your child reaches for a screen out of boredom, tiredness, or habit, try a quick redirect to one sense. One question pulls them back into their body, the room, and contact with you:

“Close your eyes — what do you hear?”

“Smell this — what does it remind you of?”

“Touch this — soft or hard?”

“Let's walk like cats.”

“Can you copy my movement?”

“What crunches when you bite?”

“Let's be a stone for 10 seconds.”

“Find something that crinkles.”

You don't have to do a big activity every time.
One question that brings your child back to their body and the room — is often enough.
