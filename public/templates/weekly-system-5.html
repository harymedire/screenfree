Welcome

Weekly System #5

This week brings nature indoors. Leaves, pebbles, water, seeds, twigs, and soil — materials that come naturally to children ages 2 to 4, but that a city apartment often keeps out of reach. This week builds:

  • sensory exploration through natural materials
  • speech and storytelling through gathered treasures
  • observation and prediction
  • patience (a seed that grows for days)
  • bare feet and contact with the earth
  • creativity with no “wrong moves”

A practical note for parents: this week needs one short walk (10–15 min) before or during the week to gather materials — leaves, pebbles, twigs, acorns. That's the only prep that's different from earlier weeks. And the walk itself becomes an activity (Day 7).

The first week with a long-lasting element:
a seed sprouts days later, a picture stays on the wall, the treasure bag comes back. Play doesn't have to be disposable — it can build something that stays.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #5 · getscreenfree.com Day 1 / 7
1

Forest on the Table

Bringing nature indoors.

We brought the forest into our room.

⚡ WOW Mission

Forest on the Table

Materials: 5–6 leaves of different shapes, 2–3 twigs, a few pebbles, acorns or chestnuts (if it's the season); a baking tray or large plate, plastic animals.

How to play

On a short walk, you and your child collect leaves, twigs, pebbles, acorns. At home, on a tray or large plate, you build a “forest” together. Add plastic animals. Your child narrates what's happening: “The bear lives here. The bunny sleeps over there. There's a river.” Help with questions: “Who's coming into the forest? Where will they sleep? Which animal is hiding?” 15 minutes of walking + an hour of play — your child sees nature differently, as a building material they get to choose.

🔧 Medium

Sorting Forest Treasure

Put everything you collected on the floor. “Put all the leaves here. All the pebbles here. All the twigs here.” Your child sorts by type, then by size: “Big leaf here, small one there.” Practices focus, fine motor skills, and concepts.

🌿 Easy

What Do We Have?

No sorting or game — just touching and naming. “This is a leaf — it rustles. This is a stone — it's heavy. This is a twig.” Plenty for younger toddlers — introduces nature through touch and words.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #5 · getscreenfree.com Day 2 / 7
2

Water and Leaf

What floats? What sinks?

Will it float, or will it sink?

⚡ WOW Mission

Float or Sink?

Materials: a large clear bowl of water; a leaf, a small stone, an ice cube, a piece of orange peel, a plastic toy, paper, a spoon.

How to play

Put the bowl of water on the table. Your child drops the items in one at a time. Before they drop each one, you ask: “What do you think about this one? Float or sink?” Your child guesses, then tests. React: “It's floating!” or “Down it went!” Use science words: “floats,” “sinks,” “drifts,” “sinks slowly, sinks fast.” This is real science packed into 5 minutes — your child practices prediction, observation, and new words. Parents are stunned at how much their child remembers.

🔧 Medium

Make Groups

After testing, sort the items: these floated, these sank. “What's different about these? Which are lighter, which are heavier?” Your child connects the result with a property of the object — the start of scientific thinking.

🌿 Easy

Water and Hand

No prediction game — your child just puts a hand in, washes, splashes. “Cold? Warm? Does the water make a sound?” Sensory exploration of water, perfect for younger toddlers or end of day.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #5 · getscreenfree.com Day 3 / 7
3

Pebble Story

Each pebble knows one word.

Each pebble knows one word.

⚡ WOW Mission

Story from Pebbles

Materials: 6–8 smooth pebbles (from a park or store-bought decorative gravel), a permanent marker.

How to play

On each pebble, draw something simple: a sun, a house, a heart, a tree, a star, an animal, a wave, a cloud. Your child pulls one at a time and you build a story together: “Once upon a time there was a sun... then a house... then a bunny...” Follow whatever your child points to — no corrections. Storytelling from scratch, no book needed. It grows speech and imagination in a way no app can match. Keep the pebbles in a little bag and use them for weeks.

🔧 Medium

Guess Which Is Missing

Lay out 4 pebbles. Your child looks at them. Then they close their eyes, you take one away: “Which one is gone?” Memory, visual focus. Use 3 pebbles for younger kids, 5–6 for older ones.

🌿 Easy

Name the Picture

No story. Your child pulls a pebble and says what they see: “Sun. House. Star.” The simplest version — practices recognizing symbols and vocabulary.

4

Seed in a Jar

Life that grows slowly.

Something is growing slowly, day by day.

⚡ WOW Mission

Life That Grows

Materials: a clear jar or glass, cotton or a paper towel, water, a few beans / chickpeas / lentils.

How to play

Put cotton (or a paper towel) at the bottom of the jar and pour in enough water to make it damp. Your child places 2–3 seeds against the glass — so you can see them. Set the jar on the windowsill. Each day your child watches and describes: “It's wet. Something's happening. The shell cracked!” Over the next few days a root appears, then a stem. An activity that “lives” for 2–3 weeks after this one. The first time your child sees what “grows slowly” really means.

🔧 Medium

Sprouting Diary

You keep a sheet of paper. Each day, draw together what you see: day 1 a seed, day 3 the first line, day 5 a stem, day 7 a leaf. Your child leads: “What do you see today?” Builds patience, observation, and a sense of time.

🌿 Easy

Drink Some Water

No drawing, just a daily ritual: “Let's check the seed. Does it need water?” Your child carefully pours in a little. A small but meaningful ritual you repeat for days.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #5 · getscreenfree.com Day 5 / 7
5

Barefoot on Grass

Feet discover the ground.

Feet know the difference.

⚡ WOW Mission

Feet Discover

Materials: if the weather allows — outside; if not — 4 plates: one with grass (or parsley), one with water, one with semolina or sand, one with pebbles.

How to play

Outside: a few minutes barefoot on grass, dirt, stone, asphalt. Name the sensation: “Grass tickles. Asphalt is hard. Sand slips between your toes.” If you can't go out, bring it in — your child steps from plate to plate and describes. A child who wears shoes all day suddenly discovers the world through their feet. Grounding in the literal sense — soothing for sensory-overloaded toddlers.

🔧 Medium

Guess Where You're Standing

Your child closes their eyes (or looks up). You move a foot to another plate. “Where are your feet now?” They guess by touch alone. Builds tactile focus and spatial awareness.

🌿 Easy

Just Grass

No path. One plate, one sensation (grass or sand). Your child touches with hand or foot. For younger toddlers, one sense of touch per session is plenty.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #5 · getscreenfree.com Day 6 / 7
6

Nature Picture

Everything you collect stays.

Everything you collect stays.

⚡ WOW Mission

A Picture That Stays

Materials: a piece of cardboard or paper, a little glue in a small dish (or double-sided tape), gathered items: leaves, petals, seeds, a pinch of sand, a twig, a feather.

How to play

Your child glues whatever they want: leaves, petals, seeds, a twig, a feather. They make a “picture” with no rules — there's no “wrong move” because everything sticks. You can add a marker for a “frame” or a signature. Hang the picture in their room and they'll show it to visitors for weeks — creativity without frustration.

🔧 Medium

All from One Thing

A picture made from one kind of material only: just leaves, just seeds. Your child picks the layout, density, direction. “Where will this one go? Here or there?” Practices composition and choice.

🌿 Easy

Carpet of Leaves

No glue. Your child arranges leaves on the floor or table however they like — like a carpet, a pile, a path. “What are you making? Where does your path lead?” Free-form, no prep.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #5 · getscreenfree.com Day 7 / 7
7

Little Walk with a Mission

A treasure hunt in nature.

Bring me treasure from the walk.

⚡ WOW Mission

Treasure Hunt

Materials: a paper bag or small pouch, a short walk (front yard, the block, to the store, or a park).

How to play

Give your child a “mission” — bring back: something round, something yellow, something that rustles, something small, something smooth. They drop everything into the bag. At home — pull them out one at a time and talk: “What round thing did you find? A chestnut! What's smooth? A stone!” An ordinary walk becomes a mission. Your child notices 10× more than usual. Save the treasure bag and look through it again over the next few days.

🔧 Medium

How Many Colors?

Walk with a question: “How many different colors can we find?” Name each one: “Yellow — see the flower over there. Green — a leaf. Brown — tree bark.” Practices focus and naming colors.

🌿 Easy

Listen to What's Outside

No collecting. You walk and listen: “What do you hear? A car? A bird? The wind?” Your child slows down to listen. Perfect for tired days or the end of the week.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #5 · getscreenfree.com SOS ideas

Ready for in-the-moment

SOS ideas
when your child asks for a screen

When your child reaches for a screen out of boredom or habit, try quickly redirecting attention to something living and natural. A look out the window, the touch of a stone, the rustle of a leaf — that brings a child back into the moment:

“Look out the window — what colors do you see?”

“Let's make a forest on the table.”

“Will this ice cube float?”

“Which stone is the warmest?”

“Give the seed a little water.”

“Listen — do you hear a bird?”

“Bring me a leaf from the treasure bag.”

“Let's go barefoot for a minute.”

Nature doesn't have to be a forest.
One leaf in a hand, one stone in a pocket — is often enough to bring your child “back into their body.”
