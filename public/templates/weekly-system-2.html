Welcome

Weekly System #2

This week is all about discovery. Through tiny secrets, shadows, foam, and footprints, your child learns what no screen can teach — how to watch, touch, listen, and truly see the world around them. Every activity is a small invitation to play with curiosity:

  • sensory exploration
  • observation and focus
  • fine motor skills
  • imagination and expression
  • curiosity and courage
  • closeness through shared discovery

You don't need fancy materials or special prep. A sock, a little soapy water, a few things from the kitchen — and suddenly everyday moments turn into mini adventures. Perfect for bath time, a quiet afternoon, or anytime your child reaches for a screen “out of boredom.”

This isn't a checklist of tasks.
This is 7 days of small discoveries — where an ordinary spoon becomes a secret, a shadow on the wall becomes a game, and the foam in the tub becomes a mission.

1

The Secret Pack

Something is inside — you have to figure it out by touch alone.

Something is inside. You can't see it. You have to find out.

⚡ WOW Mission

The Secret in Your Hand

Materials: a sock or small bag, 3–5 familiar household objects.

How to play

Hide an object inside that can't be peeked at. Your child reaches a hand in and explores slowly. Help with questions: “What do you feel?” “Is it hard or soft?” “Does any part of it move?” “Have you used this before?” When they guess: “Yes! You uncovered the secret!”

🔧 Medium

Find what I'm asking for

Put 2–3 items inside. “Find the spoon.” “Where's the car?” If they pick wrong — pop it back and try again, no correction, just play.

🌿 Easy

Pull out and name

Your child simply pulls an item out and names it. “What did you find?” “Where do we use this?” A little chat about each object.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #2 · getscreenfree.com Day 2 / 7
2

Foam Mountain

The toys have vanished — they're hiding inside fluffy clouds of foam.

The toys have vanished. They're hiding in the foam.

⚡ WOW Mission

Foam Rescue

Materials: water, a little shampoo or soap, a bowl or tub, a few toys.

How to play

Whip up thick foam by mixing water and shampoo. Hide the toys in the foam. Your child digs them out with their hands. Help with voices: “Where's the bunny?” “Can you find him?” “Feel with your hand… something's there!” When they find one: “Yes! You rescued him!”

🔧 Medium

Find and sort

Rescued toys go into a second bowl by a rule. “All small ones first.” “Now just cars.” “Now everything red.” A sorting workout wrapped in a feel-good mission.

🌿 Easy

Foam play

Your child just stirs, washes their hands, and watches the foam. No toys — pure sensory exploration. Talk about how soft, light, and melty the foam feels.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #2 · getscreenfree.com Day 3 / 7
3

The Big Sort

Everything moves from one place to another — over and over again.

Everything moves from one place to another.

⚡ WOW Mission

Move it

Materials: 2–3 bowls, a cup or spoon, water or chunky items (large pasta, blocks, bottle caps).

How to play

Your child pours or scoops the contents from one bowl to another — and back again. Rhythm and repetition are the magic. Help with voice: “Filling it up… now emptying… again!” “Is it full or empty?” “Where does it go now?” Swap bowls and tools for endless variation.

🔧 Medium

Careful pour

“Move it all into this bowl.” “Try not to spill.” You can add a beat: slow, fast, pause. Builds precision and self-control.

🌿 Easy

Just hands

No spoon or cup — just moving things by hand. Younger kids get to explore textures and hand coordination. Name what lands in their fingers.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #2 · getscreenfree.com Day 4 / 7
4

Footprint Trail

Someone walked through here. Follow the trail and see where it leads.

Someone walked through here. Follow them.

⚡ WOW Mission

A path through the house

Materials: paper or cardboard for footprints, tape, a small surprise at the end.

How to play

Trace or cut out paper footprints and tape them through the house — from the living room to the kitchen, around the table, down the hallway. Your child follows. Voice it: “Where do they lead?” “Keep going… one more!” “What's waiting at the end?” A small reward waits at the finish — a sticker, a piece of fruit, a favorite toy.

🔧 Medium

Different moves

On certain footprints, add a task: “Jump here.” “Crawl here.” “Go slow here.” “Spin here!” Change the rhythm and the trail becomes a real challenge.

🌿 Easy

Short route

Just 4–5 footprints in one room leading to a hidden toy. Fewer steps, more cheers along the way.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #2 · getscreenfree.com Day 5 / 7
5

Shadow Hunt

Something is moving on the wall — try to catch it.

Something is moving on the wall.

⚡ WOW Mission

Runaway shadows

Materials: a flashlight (or your phone light), a darkened room, a hand or toy for shadows.

How to play

Dim the room and switch on the flashlight. Make shadows with your hand (bunny, bird, dog) or a toy and slide them across the wall. Your child tries to catch them. “Will you catch it?” “Where did it run off to?” “Quick, it's going up!” Change speed and direction to keep it tricky.

🔧 Medium

Follow the shadow

Your child traces the shadow with a hand or their whole body. “If the shadow goes left, you go left.” A workout for tracking, coordination, and reaction.

🌿 Easy

Watch and follow

Younger kids just watch and follow the movement with their eyes. Name it: “Shadow's moving!” “Shadow stopped.” “Little shadow, big shadow.”

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #2 · getscreenfree.com Day 6 / 7
6

Rolling Ramp

Something's heading down. What will happen? Let's find out.

Something's heading down. What will happen?

⚡ WOW Mission

Let go and watch

Materials: books or boxes for a base, cardboard or a board as a ramp, toy cars and balls.

How to play

Build a ramp — prop a piece of cardboard on 2–3 books to make a slope, or lean it on a chair. Your child sends cars, balls, and other things down the ramp. Voice it: “Will it go fast or slow?” “Who's faster?” “Try again!” Race two cars side by side — “Who won?”

🔧 Medium

Change the ramp

Change the slope (more books = steeper) and the height. Talk through what changes: “Now it's slower.” “Now it's flying!” A first lesson in cause and effect.

🌿 Easy

One lane

A simple ramp, one ball or one car, repeat. Younger kids love the rhythm of let-go-then-fetch. “Again?” “Let's go!”

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #2 · getscreenfree.com Day 7 / 7
7

Tiny Stage

Everyone is watching. It's your turn — show us what you've got.

Everyone is watching. It's your turn.

⚡ WOW Mission

My show

Materials: a blanket or rug as the “stage,” a pillow as a chair, a few stuffed animals as the audience.

How to play

Set the scene: a blanket on the floor as a stage, a pillow to sit on, stuffed animals lined up as the crowd. Your child performs whatever they want — sings, dances, acts, tells a story. Ask: “What will you show us today?” “Who's coming to the show?” Big applause at the end, “Bravo!” You can record a quick clip on your phone for parents to watch later (briefly — no screens during play).

🔧 Medium

Quick roles

You hand out roles in turn: “Now you're the singer.” “Now you're the teacher teaching the bear.” “Now you're the doctor.” A workout for picturing yourself in different roles.

🌿 Easy

Show for the stuffed animals

No expectations from a parent — your child performs just for the toys they've lined up. A quiet show for a stuffed-animal audience, no pressure.

SCREENFREE · Weekly System #2 · getscreenfree.com SOS ideas

Ready for in-the-moment

SOS ideas
when your child asks for a screen

When you sense your child is reaching for a screen out of boredom, habit, or needing a little direction, try quickly redirecting attention to one of these mini ideas:

“Let's find something blue.”

“Can you bring me the ball?”

“Want to walk like a bear?”

“Let's build a tunnel.”

“Can you drop these into the box?”

“Want to read together?”

“Let's hide the bear and find it.”

“Will you help me fold the towels?”

You don't have to “perfectly replace the screen” every time.
It's enough to have one ready idea that redirects the moment.
